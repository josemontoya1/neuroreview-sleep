The Science of Sleep

AP Psychology SimLab Module

Sleep is not just a passive state; it's a dynamic biological process essential for survival, memory consolidation, tissue repair, and energy restoration.

The Stages of Sleep

A typical 90-minute cycle repeats 4-6 times a night, tracked via EEG.

NREM-1 (Transition)

Duration: 5-10 minutes

Brain Waves: Slowing theta waves

  • Transition from wakefulness to sleep.
  • May experience hypnic jerks (sudden muscle spasms).

NREM-2 (Light Sleep)

Duration: 45-55% of total sleep

Brain Waves: Sleep spindles & K-complexes

  • Heart rate slows and body temperature drops.
  • Brief bursts of rapid brain activity (spindles).

NREM-3 (Deep / Slow-Wave Sleep)

Brain Waves: Slow delta waves

  • Most physically restorative stage.
  • Growth hormone released; tissues repaired.
  • Sleepwalking/talking can occur here.

REM (Paradoxical Sleep)

Brain Waves: Fast, wake-like waves

  • Heart rate increases, vivid dreams occur.
  • REM atonia: Muscles paralyzed to prevent acting out dreams.
  • Essential for memory consolidation.

Theories of Sleep & Dreaming

Restorative Theory

Sleep restores your body and brain (tissue repair, memory consolidation).

Adaptive Theory

Sleep evolved to keep animals inactive and safe during vulnerable times.

Activation-Synthesis

Dreams are the brain's attempt to make sense of random neural noise during REM sleep.

Memory Consolidation

Dreams reflect the brain processing and organizing daily experiences into long-term memory.

Tip: These theories are not mutually exclusive. Activation-synthesis can coexist with memory consolidation.

Common Sleep Disorders

Insomnia

Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or getting restorative sleep.

Sleep Apnea

Breathing repeatedly stops and starts. Treated with a CPAP machine.

Narcolepsy

Irresistible sleepiness and sudden REM "sleep attacks." Involves cataplexy (muscle tone loss) and low hypocretin.

Restless Leg Syndrome

Uncomfortable sensations in the legs and an irresistible urge to move them, disrupting sleep.

NREM-3 is physically restorative. REM is for memory & vivid dreams.
Neurotransmitters

Brain Structures

Pitfall Alert: Dreams can occur in NREM (just less vivid). Also, REM is NOT the most physically restorative stage—that's NREM-3!
